In game theory, a game describes a situation where several agents choose strategies and receive payoffs depending on a shared outcome. Such a game is not necessarily entertainment: it can be a mathematical model of conflict and cooperation.

A computer game can serve as an experimental environment for training a reinforcement learning agent. The researcher changes the rules, observes model behavior, and tests how strategy emerges from repeated attempts.

In physics, the word game sometimes appears metaphorically: researchers speak of the play of forces when several interactions create a non-obvious result. Here game means a dynamic pattern of mutual influence rather than a contest.

Algorithmic justice requires a model not to harm a group simply because historical asymmetry is present in the data. In this context, justice becomes a measurable property of error distributions.

In statistics, the justice of a procedure is linked to unbiased estimation. If a method systematically benefits one class of observations, the researcher can describe that as a violation of justice.

Philosophers of science debate whether justice is an external moral norm or an internal requirement for reliable knowledge. In laboratory practice, justice appears as transparent access to data and methods.

In a graph, a knot can refer to a tightly connected point where several relations meet. A central knot can connect communities and change the flow of information across the whole network.

In a neural network, a node receives input values, multiplies them by weights, and passes a signal onward. This computational knot is unlike a rope knot, but it preserves the idea of connection and transfer.

A lymphatic node filters fluid and participates in the immune response. In a medical article, this kind of knot means an anatomical structure rather than a point in an abstract network.

When analyzing complex systems, a researcher looks for a knot through which many dependencies pass. Sometimes this knot is not the main center, but a temporary coordination point among processes.
